# X002 "The Missing First Job" — sources (checked 2 Nov 2026)

## 1. The 19% gap · hero number
- **Primary (read):** Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "No Widespread Displacement, but the AI Employment Gap for Young Workers Has Widened to 19%", 12 Aug 2026 — https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/
  - Workers **aged 22–25** in **highly AI-exposed occupations** (the two most exposed quintiles) are **about 19% below** where their employment would be had it kept pace with similarly aged workers in less-exposed occupations.
  - Was **15%** at the July 2025 data vintage → **19%** as of June 2026.
  - "The adjustment appears to operate primarily through **reduced hiring** of young workers rather than increased separations."
  - "**Experienced workers show no comparable gap**."
  - Data: **ADP payroll data**; exposure from Eloundou et al. (2024). Paper: "Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence" (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen), revised 12 Aug 2026; monthly Canaries Dashboard with ADP Research.
- **Authors' caveats (must stay true in the video):** "descriptive patterns, not causal estimates of the effect of AI"; gaps shrink when education is accounted for; some trends started before generative AI; ADP gaps larger than national survey benchmarks.

## 2. Context (optional on screen)
